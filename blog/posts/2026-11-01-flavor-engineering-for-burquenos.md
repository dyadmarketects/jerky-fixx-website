Hi, it's Aaliyah. People always ask how we come up with 25 flavors. The honest answer is that we make jerky for Burqueños, and Burqueños have a very specific palate. So today I'm pulling back the curtain on how we build flavors, and I get to introduce one my mom is really proud of: Hot Mamma Cheeto.

## Cooking for the Burque palate

If you grew up in Albuquerque, you already know the rules. Chile goes on everything. "Red or green?" is a real question, and "Christmas" is a real answer. You probably had Takis or Hot Cheetos with lime after school, a pickle from the corner store and Tajin on your mango. Sweet, sour, salty and spicy all in one bite is just normal here.

It goes beyond snacks, too. Think about a plate of enchiladas smothered in green, a bowl of posole with red chile on the side, or roasted chile in the air every fall when the roasters fire up outside the grocery stores. Food here is bold and it's personal. Everybody has a favorite spot, a favorite heat and strong opinions about both.

That's the palate we cook for. Our base is always the same: small batch carne seca, thin cut beef, a heavy hand on the seasoning and a long, slow dry. What changes is how we stack flavors on top of it.

## How we engineer a flavor

We don't have a lab coat, but we do have a process.

1. **Start with what Burque already loves.** Green chile, red chile, lime, garlic, Hot Cheetos, pickles and Tajin all come straight from the snacks people grew up on.
2. **Pick a heat level on purpose.** Every flavor gets a number from 0 to 5, so a Honey Teriyaki fan and an X-Hot Green fiend can both find their bag.
3. **Balance the bite.** Heat needs something to play against. That's why you'll see lime with chile, dill with Hot Cheeto and honey with teriyaki.
4. **Make it crunch and cling.** Thin cut carne seca holds onto seasoning, so dusts like Hot Cheeto and Takis really coat every strip.
5. **Test it on family and customers.** If people at the booth keep coming back for another sample, it earns a spot on the menu.

That's how you end up with flavors like **X-Mas** (red and green chile together), **Tajin Limon**, **Takis** and **Hot Cheeto Pickle**. Some of them sound wild on paper. They sell out anyway.

## Meet Hot Mamma Cheeto

Hot Mamma Cheeto is what happens when you take two fan favorites and turn them up. **Hot Mamma** is our bold red chile flavor with a serious kick. **Hot Cheetos** is red hot Cheeto dust packed into every strip. Put them together and you get Hot Mamma Cheeto, sitting at heat level 5, right up there with X-Hot Green.

It's double trouble for spice fiends: deep red chile heat first, then that Hot Cheeto crunch and tang that keeps you reaching back into the bag. My mom, Sabrina, is the Hot Mamma behind it, so here she is to introduce it herself.

## Why small batch matters for bold flavors

Big brands have to make one flavor taste the same in every state. We don't. Because we make small batches here in Albuquerque, we can season heavy, tweak a recipe when customers tell us what they want, and keep a flavor that only makes sense to people from here. That's also why we can say yes to custom requests. If there's a combo you've been dreaming about, ask us at the next event or send us a message.
