Hi, it's Aaliyah. Every year around now my phone starts filling up with the same question from friends: "What do I get my dad?" Or my boss. Or my aunt who already owns everything. And every year my answer is pretty much the same. Get them jerky. Nobody I know has ever been sad to open a bag of carne seca, and a few bags from a small family business here in Albuquerque says a lot more than another candle.

So here's the gift guide I keep texting people anyway, organized by who you're shopping for.

## The basics before you shop

- **Sizes:** small bags are $7 and large bags are $20. Small for stocking stuffers, large for the people you really like.
- **Flavors:** we make 25 of them, rated for heat from 0 to 5. You can see every one on the [jerky menu](/jerky/).
- **Paying:** Cash App, Venmo, Apple Pay or cash. Nothing gets paid online.
- **Getting it there:** we text to confirm your order, then our dealer drops it at the door in Albuquerque or Rio Rancho and sends you a photo once it's delivered.

My best tip for gifting is to mix it up. Grab a few different flavors instead of three of the same, so whoever opens it gets to try a little of everything.

## For the coworker

You don't always know what coworkers like, so I go mild and crowd friendly. A small bag each of [Garlic Parm](/jerky/garlic-parm/), [Honey Teriyaki](/jerky/honey-teriyaki/) and [Lemon Pepper](/jerky/lemon-pepper/) covers a lot of taste buds. Garlic Parm and Honey Teriyaki are both a 0 on our heat scale, and Lemon Pepper is a 1, so nobody's eyes are watering at their desk.

Doing a whole team? Small $7 bags make it easy to give everybody their own. For more mild ideas, my post on [mild jerky for kids, grandmas and picky eaters](/blog/mild-beef-jerky/) goes through all eight of our 0 and 1 flavors.

## For the host

Going to a holiday party, a Friendsgiving or a family dinner where somebody else is cooking? Skip the bottle everyone else brings and show up with a large bag or two for the table.

My pick is a large Honey Teriyaki, which we call the gateway flavor for the kids and the grandmas, plus a large [Hot Cheeto Pickle](/jerky/hot-cheeto-pickle/). That one's dill pickle tang with hot cheeto heat, and it sounds wild, but it's a 3 on the heat scale and it starts conversations. Put both out next to the chips and watch which bag empties first.

## For the New Mexico purist

You know this person. Green chile on everything, and red or green is a serious question. For them, it has to be chile.

Start with **Green**, our classic New Mexico green chile carne seca, and **Red**, red chile beef jerky with that deep, smoky flavor. Both are a 3. Then add **X-Mas**, which is red and green chile together, Christmas style, the way New Mexico does it. It's also a 3, and it might be the most New Mexico gift you can give in December.

Want to see all the chile flavors in one place? Check out our [green chile jerky](/green-chile-jerky/) page, or read my [green chile jerky ranked by heat](/blog/green-chile-jerky-ranked-by-heat/) post if you're trying to pick the right level.

## For the heat chaser

Every family has one. Don't you dare give them a 0.

Go straight for [X-Hot Green](/jerky/x-hot-green/), our hottest green chile jerky and a 5. Pair it with **Hot Mamma Cheeto**, also a 5, which is Hot Mamma heat plus hot cheeto dust. If you want to round it out with something sweet that still burns, [Mango Habanero](/jerky/mango-habanero/) is a 4 with sweet mango up front and a slow burn on the way out.

I wrote a whole post on [spicy jerky for heat chasers](/blog/spicy-jerky-for-heat-chasers/) if you want to go deeper. Just maybe include a glass of milk with the gift.

## For the snack aisle kid

Teens, cousins, the little brother who lives on chips. These are the flavors that make them do a double take. [Hot Cheetos](/jerky/hot-cheetos/) packs red hot cheeto dust into every strip, and [Takis](/jerky/takis/) brings that rolled chile lime crunch flavor. Both are a 4, so if your snack kid is on the younger side, **Tajin Limon** is a gentler 2 with chile lime and a bright limon finish.

## For the homesick cousin

This one's close to my heart. If you've got family who moved away and misses home, a bag of New Mexico chile jerky hits different. Our door drop off covers Albuquerque and Rio Rancho, so here's how I'd do it: order a few bags to your place before they visit for the holidays and send them back with a stash, or tuck them in a box you're already putting together for them. Green, Red or X-Mas would be my picks, because nothing says home like chile.

## Want something nobody else has?

If you're shopping for the person who has everything, a custom flavor could be the move. My family makes custom, by request batches when they can, but they take extra time, so reach out early. I explain how it works in my post on [custom jerky flavors by request](/blog/custom-jerky-flavors-by-request/).
