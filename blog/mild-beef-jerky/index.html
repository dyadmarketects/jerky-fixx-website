Mild Jerky for Kids, Grandmas and Picky Eaters

By Aaliyah Penn /November 17, 2026 /5 min read

Posted November 17, 2026

Hi, it's Aaliyah. Last week I wrote a whole post for the heat chasers, the people who walk up to our booth and ask for the hottest thing we make. This week is for everybody else. The kids. The grandmas. The friend who says "is this spicy?" before every single bite. The uncle who swears green chile is too much for him even though he lives in New Mexico.

Good news for all of you: mild beef jerky doesn't have to be boring. Some of our most popular flavors sit at a 0 or a 1 on our heat scale, and they still bring a ton of flavor. Here's the full mild lineup and how I'd match each one to the people in your life.

How our heat scale works

Every Jerky Fixx flavor gets a number from 0 to 5. A 5 is our hottest. A 0 means no heat at all. A 1 has a tiny bit of pepper or tang, but nothing that's going to make anybody reach for a glass of milk.

Out of our 25 flavors, five are a 0 and three are a 1. That's eight flavors for anyone who wants all the beefy, savory goodness without the burn. If you've ever wondered what makes our jerky different in the first place, my plain English guide to carne seca explains the thin cut style we're known for.

The zeros: all flavor, no heat

Honey Teriyaki

If I had to pick one flavor for a picky eater, this is it. Our menu calls it "the gateway flavor for the kids and the grandmas," and that's exactly how it works at our table. It's sweet, sticky and savory, and it's one of our top sellers. A lot of people start here and then slowly work their way across the menu. Some never leave, and that's fine too.

Teriyaki

Savory teriyaki glaze on tender, thin cut beef. Think of it as Honey Teriyaki's less sweet sibling. If someone in your house likes teriyaki but isn't big on sugary snacks, this is the move.

Garlic Parm

Roasted garlic and nutty parmesan. Mellow on the heat, loud on the flavor. Garlic Parm is one of our top sellers, and I think that's because it works for everybody. Spice lovers grab it as a break between hot bites. Mild fans grab it because it's just plain good. It's an easy pick when you're buying for a group and don't know everyone's taste.

Garlic

Garlic lovers only. Big savory garlic flavor with zero heat. This one is for the person who orders extra garlic on everything. No cheese, no sweetness, just garlic and beef.

Salt

Simple, beefy and clean. Just meat and salt for the purists. Salt is perfect for the pickiest eaters, the ones who pull every topping off their pizza. There's nothing to pick off here. It's also a nice way to taste what our carne seca is like on its own before you start exploring seasonings.

The ones: a little pepper, a little tang

Lemon Pepper

Bright lemon and cracked black pepper. A classic that never gets old. Lemon Pepper is another top seller, and it's a great choice for grandparents and parents who like something with a little zip but don't want chile heat. The pepper is there, but it's gentle.

Salt x Pepper

The classic pair. Salt and cracked black pepper on thin cut beef. If Salt feels too plain and Lemon Pepper feels too bright, Salt x Pepper lands right in the middle. Simple and familiar.

Pickle

Tangy dill pickle jerky. Sour, salty and weirdly addictive. Every family has a pickle person. You know the one. They drink the juice out of the jar. This flavor is for them. It's a 1 because of the tang, not because of any chile. (If your pickle person likes heat too, Hot Cheeto Pickle is a 3, but that's a story for another day.)

Matching flavors to the people you're feeding

Here's how I'd break it down if you're shopping for someone specific.

  • For little kids: Honey Teriyaki first, then Teriyaki or Salt. Sweet and simple usually wins.
  • For grandmas and grandpas: Lemon Pepper, Salt x Pepper or Garlic Parm. Classic flavors with a little personality.
  • For the pickiest eater you know: Salt. Nothing surprising, nothing to pick off.
  • For garlic fans: Garlic if they want it straight, Garlic Parm if they like it a little richer.
  • For the sour snack crowd: Pickle, no question.

If you're feeding a big group with mixed heat tolerance, like at a party or a ball game, my game day flavor guide has ideas for mixing mild bags with some hotter ones so nobody gets left out.

Try a mild sampler

My favorite way to find a mild favorite is to grab a few small bags and taste them side by side. A small bag is $7 and a large bag is $20, so small bags make it easy to try three or four flavors without committing to one. Once you know which one the kids keep stealing, go big on that one next time.
